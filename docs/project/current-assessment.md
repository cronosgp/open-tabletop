# Project assessment and tactical consolidation plan

Assessment date: 24 October 2026. This is a review of the current working tree and recorded checks, not a release certification. Use the [support matrix](support-matrix.md) for runtime boundaries and the [training checkpoint](../guides/tactical-training.md) for exact commands and latest integration results.

## Where the project stands

The project is an integrated prototype with substantial local rules coverage and a playable, bounded Marine training scenario. It has progressed beyond isolated movement and attack demonstrations: preparation, deployment, phase progression, interruptions, attack resolution, scoring and persistence can participate in one match lifecycle. It has not yet demonstrated a dependable two-person product across the intended computers and release platforms.

The strongest evidence is local and scenario-specific. The largest remaining gap is the distance between implementing many individual rules and proving that players can discover, understand and complete their interactions in an ordinary match. Catalog breadth, assertions, graphical captures, loopback, separate-machine play and human comprehension answer different questions.

Do not assign a single project completion percentage. The roadmap includes independent rules, content, usability, networking and release gates, with no validated common denominator. The 85 selectable shared-book Marine identities measure catalog admission, not 85 fully certified implementations or general army legality.

## Weak points and practical consequences

| Weak point | Consequence | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Uneven integration evidence | Individual rule tests can pass while a full match stalls or reconnects inconsistently. | Make the bounded training lifecycle the integration target, with actual public commands and explicit checkpoint evidence. |
| Separate-machine recovery is unproven | Loopback now passes, but a real LAN disconnect/rejoin can still expose reachability, timing or platform differences. | Keep the bounded loopback gate green, then run the same scenario on two machines before claiming LAN readiness. |
| Discoverability is not established by automation | A legal choice may remain difficult to find or its cost/owner may be misunderstood. | Observe people using the actual UI. Captures can expose clipping and hierarchy, but cannot establish comprehension. |
| Asymmetric forces and free play | Useful learning opportunities are possible, but particular abilities may never become legal; unequal points do not imply competitive balance. | Explain the exploratory purpose, provide side swapping and tips, and record absent opportunities during playtest. Do not force rolls or grant tutorial-only CP. |
| A large root controller remains | UI, RPC endpoints, persistence adapters and rendering still share a large change surface. | Extract only demonstrated seams after behavior is characterized; avoid a broad rewrite during scenario stabilization. |
| Evidence is spread across historical reports | Old counts and partial passes can be mistaken for present release support. | Keep one dated checkpoint and trace exact commands/reports. Supersede claims narrowly rather than erasing failed attempts. |
| Distribution is less validated than local development | A PCK startup does not prove a native binary, tactical content availability or another OS; [remote workflow run 17](https://github.com/cronosgp/open-tabletop/actions/runs/39658206740) failed before tests on the older merge revision because it expected an ignored legacy snapshot. | Keep the data-free baseline green, rerun remote CI on the corrected revision, then check the actual native artifact and platform explicitly. Keep third-party exports/derived data out of Git and distributed packages. |
| Cleanup diagnostics in historical or UI-heavy roles | The exercised tactical/UI/full-demo paths now release cleanly, but broader roles may still expose renderer/ObjectDB lifecycle issues. | Keep the clean current gates green, then audit remaining UI-heavy roles before a release-quality claim. |

## Initial-match design decision

Use **Fire and Manoeuvre** (Captain, five Intercessors, five Hellblasters; 290 runtime points) against **Close Assault** (Chaplain, five Assault Intercessors, three Bladeguard Veterans; 235 runtime points). Both use current admitted abilities and Gladius/Core stratagems. The title screen shows the forces, totals and exploratory limit; players may swap sides.

This is a free-play training match, not a mandatory tutorial sequence. The Help screen now offers phase-labelled optional experiments for a legal leader ability, a CP stratagem, standard/supercharged plasma with Hazardous and the Bladeguard stance choice, and marks a leader/CP/Bladeguard opportunity only when the live command projection offers it. The normal preparation, CP costs, timing, targets and dice remain authoritative. Attach the Captain to Intercessors and the Chaplain to Assault Intercessors through the existing preparation controls. Default equipment keeps the initial decision load bounded. The 43 × 43-inch custom training mission and five-round length remain explicit; there is no competitive balance or full official legality claim.

Keep the former mirror as a regression configuration and saved-list default. Preserve the clean-checkout movement demo without external downloads. See the [configuration](../guides/tactical-training.md) for exact preset/scenario identities and options.

The first guided-session feedback now identifies a separate P0/P1 UX correction set: bounded responsive guidance, a single primary decision surface, defender-owned legal allocation, explicit Command Re-roll pass choices, structured movement rejection reasons and deliberately paced player-facing dice with anticipation and impact beats. The full issue list and acceptance gates are in the [tactical training UX audit](tactical-training-ux-audit.md). These are not treated as fixed by the existing headless or loopback passes.

## Scope held for this increment

No new faction, broad API catalog, battlefield editor, mandatory tutorial scripting or generalized reaction-engine rewrite is needed to consolidate this scenario. Those remain roadmap choices after the reliability and player-experience gates. A specific blocker may justify a smaller dependency change, but does not justify claiming unrelated roadmap completion.

The next product milestone is **a reliable, understandable, resumable tactical training match**, with the exact validated configuration and platforms stated. It is not general 11th-edition completeness. Any unresolved progression, save loss, misleading required choice or ownership defect blocks that milestone.
